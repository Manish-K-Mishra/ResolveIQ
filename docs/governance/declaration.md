# Declaration (Governance Framework §6)

Written last, from the measured runs in `evaluation/results/final_*`, the
[risk register](risk_register.md), the [fairness audit](fairness_audit.md) and the
[incident runbook](incident_response.md). Acceptance is **to be confirmed by the project owner**.

| Statement | Our position |
|---|---|
| **This system must never …** | …send an automatic answer to a ticket that staff must handle: a security incident, a compliance or legal request, a breach, exposed credentials, a refund or chargeback, a feature request, an unclear request, or a ticket that tries to instruct the system. It must also never send an answer that contains private data, cites a document not approved for the ticket's intent, or promises a refund or a timeline. |
| **The mechanism that enforces that is …** | Deterministic Python, not a model. (1) Routing policy RP-1.0 (`route.py`, `config/routing_policy.json`) escalates restricted intents and sensitive matters, blocks injection, and needs confidence ≥ 0.70 and an approved document scoring ≥ 0.40. A missing confidence counts as 0. (2) The pre-send guardrails (`guardrails.py` `validate_response` and `validate_generated`) block private data, ungrounded or non-verbatim quotes, citations outside the approved set, and commitments. (3) Automatic sending is **off by default**. The runtime kill switch (`python -m cloudserve.killswitch on`) holds every answer from the next ticket on, with no deployment. (4) Every decision is logged with its reason, policy, prompt and requirement ids, and the log reconciles with the tickets processed. Measured: 0 of 14 `must_not_auto_respond` tickets answered in each of the three validation runs, 0 of 87 on development, and 80/80 and 500/500 decisions logged. |
| **The most likely way it could still cause harm is …** | **A correct-looking answer from the right document that does not fit the customer's case.** About 1 in 5 offline automatic answers goes to a ticket the label says should have gone to a person (auto precision 0.7879; 0.8947 with Claude Haiku 4.5). A second route is a **stale approved article** quoted faithfully: there is no freshness control (R-05). A third is **unequal service**: long tickets are answered automatically far less often (21.6% against 58.1% for short ones) because of how the lexical retrieval score is normalised. The fairness target of under 5 points is not met. Calibration, human answer quality and behaviour under load are not yet measured or not yet within target. |
| **We would not deploy this without first …** | (1) Completing the two-assessor human review of `human_review_sample.csv` (hallucination rate, citation check, satisfaction proxy). (2) A pilot with automatic sending **off**, where agents approve held answers, before any release. (3) A freshness signal on the documentation, with a re-approval step in `intent_documents`. (4) A length-robust retrieval score re-derived on development data, then the fairness audit re-run. (5) A timed kill-switch drill by an operator unfamiliar with the system, and a load test. (6) An evaluation on tickets not duplicated in development: the hidden set was never available, and 62 of 80 validation bodies are copies. (7) The project owner's sign-off on the residual risks. |
